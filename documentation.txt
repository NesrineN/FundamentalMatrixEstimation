Is 67-85% good enough, or should you expect higher?
This is the right question to ask next. A few things affect what "good" looks like for TUM Freiburg1 specifically:

Freiburg1 sequences have relatively small baselines between consecutive frames (it's a handheld indoor sequence, not a wide-baseline stereo rig) — small baseline means more points sit close to weak-parallax/near-epipole configurations, which naturally produces more borderline cheirality failures even with a perfect pipeline. So values in the 70-85% range for this dataset aren't necessarily a red flag.
Uncorrected lens distortion (mentioned last time, still worth checking) would systematically bias point positions in a way that degrades cheirality margins uniformly, without causing catastrophic failures — consistent with what you're seeing now (no more huge outliers, but still meaningful minority failing).
The lowest current result (67%, first pair) is worth a quick individual look — check whether that particular pair has a shorter baseline / more rotation-dominant motion than the others, which would explain why it sits at the low end of the new, otherwise-tighter band.
